## Why Surface Tests Fall Short on High-Value Gold

When a kilo bar, doré shipment or wholesale parcel of 22K jewelry changes hands in the **Dubai Gold Souq**, a difference of just one part per thousand (1.0‰) represents significant money. Hand-held X-ray fluorescence (XRF) spectrometers and touchstone acid tests only inspect the outer few microns of a piece. Thick electroplating, surface enrichment or an inhomogeneous melt can easily mislead a surface reading.

**Fire assay (cupellation)** solves this by testing a homogenized sample through high-temperature pyro-metallurgical separation and wet-chemical parting. Tested against ISO 11426 protocols, it remains the undisputed benchmark used by central banks, LBMA-accredited refiners and hallmarking laboratories worldwide.

## Inside the 5-Stage Cupellation Procedure

At **Precise Gold Laboratory L.L.C.** on the ground floor of the Regal Sky Gold building, every Fire Assay follows five controlled stages:

### 1. Homogenization & Analytical Weighing

First, if the lot consists of mixed scrap or rough cast bars, it is melted in an induction furnace so the alloy is uniform throughout. Drillings or pin samples (~250 mg to 500 mg) are weighed on a sub-milligram analytical balance with draft-shield protection.

### 2. Inquartation & Lead Fusion

To allow nitric acid to later dissolve the non-gold fraction cleanly, the gold-to-silver ratio must be adjusted to roughly 1 part gold to 2.5–3 parts silver—a classical step known as **inquartation**. The weighed sample and pure silver addition are wrapped together in pure, bismuth-free lead foil.

### 3. Cupellation at 1,050 °C

The lead packet is placed onto a pre-heated porous magnesia **cupel** inside our muffle furnace at approximately 1,050 °C. In an oxidizing atmosphere, the lead melts and oxidizes into litharge (PbO), carrying copper, zinc, nickel and other base metals into the porous body of the cupel. Only the noble metals—gold and silver—remain on the cupel surface as a bright spherical doré bead.

### 4. Rolling & Nitric Acid Parting

Once cooled, the doré bead is flattened on a steel anvil, annealed and rolled into a thin strip that is loosely coiled into a **cornet**. The cornet is boiled in successive baths of laboratory-grade nitric acid inside graduated parting flasks. The acid dissolves all silver into silver nitrate solution while leaving the porous gold cornet intact.

### 5. Annealing & Gravimetric Fineness Calculation

After thorough washing in deionized water, the cornet is annealed in the furnace until it regains the dense, bright metallic luster of pure gold. A final weighing on our micro-balance against Simultaneous Control Check (SCC) reference standards yields the exact fineness in parts per thousand (‰)—for example, **995.0‰**, **916.6‰ (22K)**, or **750.0‰ (18K)**—with repeatability inside ±0.1‰ to ±0.2‰.
