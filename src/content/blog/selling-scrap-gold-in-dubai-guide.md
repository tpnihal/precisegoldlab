## Why Dubai Gold Souq Is the Premier Market for Selling Scrap Gold

Deira’s **Dubai Gold Souq** operates on some of the tightest physical bullion spreads in the world. However, whether you are liquidating broken 18K chains, old 22K wedding bangles, manufacturing sweeps or mixed-karat trade parcels, the price you walk away with depends on two numbers: **verified net weight** and **certified fineness (‰)**.

Here is how seasoned traders protect their payout—and how **Precise Gold Laboratory L.L.C.** ensures every seller receives transparent, laboratory-backed value.

## Step 1: Separate Non-Gold Components Before Melting

Gemstones, watch movements, steel springs inside clasps, and non-gold solder add gross weight but zero gold value. Before a mixed scrap parcel is valued for final settlement:

1. **Stone Removal:** Diamonds and coloured stones should be unmounted so they are not damaged in the furnace and do not distort pre-melt weight.
2. **Pre-Melt Weighing:** Your parcel is weighed openly on a calibrated balance (`0.01 g` to `0.001 g` precision) under CCTV at our ground-floor reception counter.

## Step 2: Induction Melting for True Homogeneity

When a parcel contains dozens of chains, rings and clasps from different manufacturers, testing just one link is a gamble—solder joints always run lower in karat than the main wire.

By melting the entire lot in a high-frequency **induction furnace** at over 1,150 °C, every piece fuses into a single homogeneous cast bar. Once poured and cooled, a small drill or pin sample represents the exact average purity of the entire parcel.

## Step 3: Fire Assay Fineness vs. "Karat Deductions"

Many street buyers who rely on scratch stones or surface XRF protect their margin by applying an arbitrary 1-karat to 2-karat "solder deduction" across your entire lot. On a 200-gram parcel of 22K gold, an unnecessary 1-karat deduction costs you over 8 grams of fine gold.

A **Fire Assay (cupellation) test** eliminates arbitrary deductions:

- It reports your bar’s exact fineness to a tenth of a part per thousand (for example, `914.8‰` instead of rounding down to `875‰ / 21K`).
- Your payout is calculated mathematically: **Post-Melt Bar Weight × Assayed Fineness (‰) × Live Fine Gold Rate**.
